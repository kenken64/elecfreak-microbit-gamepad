Gamepad Firmware Blocks

What the micro:bit gamepad firmware does, drawn the way MakeCode draws a program. It's a picture for reading, not a program you can load: the real firmware is C++ in src/v1/, running on Nordic's Bluetooth stack. Yellow notes explain each part.

P0stick left / right P1stick up / down P2keys 1–6 (one wire) P5button A P11button B

When it switches on

The self-test pulls each pin down, then up, with the chip's own resistors. A pin the Joystick:bit drives barely moves; a pin with a loose contact swings to 0 and 1023. That's how the P1 fault showed up.
The stick is assumed to be centred at power-on. If it reads more than 10 % off-centre, 2047 (the middle of 0–4095) is used instead.
Hold A + B while switching on to forget every paired PC. The big X confirms it.

All six keys share one wire, P2. Each key pulls it to a different voltage, and the limits sit halfway between them. Your board measured 1, 512, 681, 766, 816, 849 for keys 1 to 6, and 1017 with nothing pressed.
Because the keys share a wire, only one key at a time can be read. A and B have their own pins, so they combine with anything.
If P1 loses contact, A becomes up and B becomes down. The stick takes over again once the contact has held for a full second.
Only changes are sent over Bluetooth, so a gamepad at rest stays quiet.

Bluetooth events

v1_ble.cpp · on_ble_evt()
on Bluetooth connected
ask the PC to encrypt the link
on pairing request
pair with Just Works (no PIN) and bond
on pairing complete
save the PC's key in flash slot 1–4

save that setting with the PC's key
on known PC reconnects
restore its saved settings
on Bluetooth disconnected
advertise micro:bit Gamepad
Every gamepad setting needs an encrypted link, so Windows pairs and remembers the gamepad on first connect.
Up to 4 PCs are remembered. A fifth replaces the one used longest ago.
Windows doesn't switch reports back on after a reconnect. The firmware restores the saved setting itself, which is why the gamepad works straight after you switch it on.

The stick reads 0–4095. A dead zone of 80 around the centre stops a resting stick from drifting, and the last 8 % of travel counts as full.
The LED pictures are the real ones from the firmware: rings while waiting, a square while pairing, a tick when ready, then a dot that follows the stick.
